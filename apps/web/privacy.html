Privacy policy

Your typing stays on your phone.

This policy explains, plainly, what FeatherKey does and does not do with your data. The short version: it stays on your device.

Effective 26 July 2026 · version 1

In one paragraph. FeatherKey is an Android keyboard that runs entirely on your device. It requests no internet permission, so it cannot send your data anywhere. There is no account, no analytics, and nothing is sold or shared. If you switch on learning, a personal word list is built and encrypted on your phone — and you can erase it at any time.

Who this applies to

This policy covers the FeatherKey keyboard app for Android. It does not cover other apps you type into, or the separate system services your phone provides (for example, its voice-recognition service — see below).

What FeatherKey processes on your device

To function as a keyboard, FeatherKey necessarily receives the keystrokes and text you enter in the field you are typing into. This processing happens only on your device, in memory, to decode taps, correct typos, and offer suggestions.

If — and only if — you turn on on-device learning, FeatherKey additionally saves, to an encrypted store on your phone:

  • words and short word pairs you type often;
  • tap-accuracy statistics used to place key targets more precisely.

It does not store your messages, full sentences, or anything you type into fields the system marks as sensitive — such as password and payment fields, which are always skipped before any keystroke is observed.

What FeatherKey never does

  • No transmission. The app holds no internet permission, so it is technically incapable of uploading, syncing, or sending your typing, learned data, or any other information off the device.
  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for and no identifier tied to you.
  • No analytics or tracking. Nothing about your usage is measured, profiled, or reported.
  • No sale or sharing. Your data is not sold, rented, or shared with anyone — there is no mechanism by which it could be.

Voice input and the microphone

The microphone key is optional. When you tap it, FeatherKey hands off to your phone's own speech-recognition service to convert speech to text. That system service is provided by your device or its vendor (for example, Google), and its own privacy policy governs how it handles the audio — which may include sending audio off the device.

FeatherKey itself does not record, keep, or transmit audio, and it uses the microphone for no purpose other than starting that voice-typing hand-off when you ask for it.

Permissions

  • Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO) — used only for the optional voice-typing key described above. If you never use voice input, it is never used.
  • No internet permission — deliberately absent, so the app cannot make network requests.

Your choices and controls

  • Learning is off by default. You are asked once, on first launch; the keyboard works fully whether or not you ever turn it on.
  • Turn it off any time. The same switch in Settings stops all personalization immediately.
  • Erase everything. Settings → "Clear learned data" removes the encrypted store at once. Uninstalling the app also removes it.

Data retention

Anything FeatherKey learns lives only on your device and only for as long as you keep it — until you clear the learned data or uninstall the app. Because nothing is transmitted, there are no server copies or backups held by us.

Children

FeatherKey collects no personal data off the device from anyone, including children. All processing is local to the device on which it is installed.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new effective date. Material changes to how the app handles data would ship with a new app version.
